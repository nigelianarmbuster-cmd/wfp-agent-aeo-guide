What AEO is

Optimizing so that AI answer engines name, cite, or recommend you when someone asks a question.

Answer engine optimization (AEO) is the practice of shaping your online presence so that AI assistants — ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, and Google AI Overviews — can find you, understand you, and mention you in the answers they generate.

The shift is in how clients search. Instead of scrolling a page of links, an increasingly large share of buyers and sellers open an assistant and ask a full question out loud: "Who is the best real estate agent in Georgetown?" or "Find me a luxury agent in Northwest Washington DC." The assistant returns a synthesized answer — often naming a handful of agents and linking to the sources it drew from.

If you are not in that answer, you are invisible at the exact moment a client is deciding who to call. AEO is the work of making sure you are one of the names and sources the assistant can see and trust.

Unlike advertising, there is no placement to buy inside an AI answer. Visibility is earned by being findable, clearly described, and consistently corroborated by outside sources — which is why the rest of this guide focuses on fundamentals you control.

AEO vs SEO

They are not opposites. Google states plainly that optimizing for AI features is still SEO.

A side-by-side comparison of what changes and what does not.
Dimension Traditional SEO AEO
Goal Rank a page high in a list of results. Be included, cited, or recommended inside a synthesized answer.
What the user sees A page of blue links they choose between. A written answer, usually naming a few sources or people.
Key signals Relevance, links, technical health, content quality. The same, plus machine-readable facts, entity consistency, and third-party corroboration.
How success is measured Rankings, impressions, clicks, sessions. Presence in answers, position among competitors, citations, recommendations.

They are not opposites. Google's guidance is that AI features on Search are governed by the same foundational requirements as regular search: helpful, people-first content plus technical eligibility. Google's own documentation says that AEO and GEO (generative engine optimization) remain SEO, and that no special schema markup or files are required to appear in AI Overviews. Treat AEO as an added layer of discipline on top of sound SEO — not a replacement for it.

How answer engines produce an answer

Three plain steps, plus one idea worth knowing: query fan-out.

  1. Crawl and index. A crawler visits pages on the open web and stores what it finds. If a bot is blocked — by robots.txt, a firewall, or a login wall — that content cannot be used.
  2. Retrieve relevant passages (RAG). When a question arrives, the system searches its index and pulls back the passages most likely to help answer it. This approach is called retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG. The answer is grounded in retrieved text, not invented from memory alone.
  3. Generate an answer with citations. The model writes a response from those passages and points back to the sources it used.

One more idea: query fan-out. Rather than searching your exact question once, an AI system often breaks it into several related sub-queries, retrieves for each, and combines the results. That is why a single page rarely wins an answer on its own — a spread of consistent, well-matched content across the web is what carries you into the response.

For Google Search specifically, Google has described this retrieval-and-fan-out pattern in its guidance on optimizing for generative AI features. (See sources 1 and 2 below.)

The 8 principles of AEO

Each principle is tagged Required for eligibility or Helps, with the source it rests on.

  1. Be crawlable by the bots that feed answers

    If the crawlers cannot read your pages, no amount of good content will surface. Allow Googlebot, OAI-SearchBot (ChatGPT search), Claude-SearchBot (Claude search), and PerplexityBot — and check that your robots.txt and any firewall or WAF rules are not blocking them.

    Required for eligibility · Sources: OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, Google

  2. Answer the question directly

    Use question-style headings and give a concise answer near the top of the page, before the supporting detail. Retrieved passages are short, so the cleanest direct answer is the one most likely to be picked up.

    Helps · Source: Google - Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content

  3. Make your facts machine-readable

    Structured data such as schema.org RealEstateAgent, Person, and FAQPage (JSON-LD is Google's recommended format) helps search engines understand your page and can enable rich results. Google states this is not required to appear in AI features.

    Helps · Sources: Google - Intro to structured data; Schema.org - RealEstateAgent

  4. Keep your entity facts consistent everywhere

    Your name, title, brokerage, license, service areas, and phone should match across every surface — your wfp.com bio, personal site, Google Business Profile, Zillow, LinkedIn, and more. Consistent facts help machines recognize you as one person, not several.

    Helps · Source: Google - Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content

  5. Earn mentions, citations, and reviews from other sources

    Third-party corroboration carries weight. When other reputable sites mention you, link to you, or carry your reviews, the assistant has independent signals that you are real, relevant, and recommended — not just a self-description.

    Helps · Source: Google - Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content

  6. Show first-hand expertise

    Original neighborhood knowledge, your own market data, and your sold and listing history are things no one else can copy. Google's people-first guidance emphasizes experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trust — with trust as the most important.

    Helps · Source: Google - Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content

  7. Keep it fresh and accurate

    Update your bios, listings, and service-area details when things change. Stale or conflicting facts are harder for an assistant to trust, and errors can persist long after you have fixed the underlying reality.

    Helps · Source: Google - Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content

  8. Measure and iterate

    Run a fixed set of questions across the engines on a schedule and track how answers change over time. Trends over several snapshots are far more meaningful than any single result. Google's own guidance frames this as ongoing optimization, not a one-time fix.

    Helps · Source: Google - Optimizing your website for generative AI features

Volatility and what to expect

What to expect

Answers vary by engine, by model version, by user, by location, and by the day you ask. The same question can produce different names an hour apart.

A set of ten queries is a smoke test, not a measurement. Use it to spot direction and trends across snapshots, and resist reading too much into any single result — good or bad.

No one can guarantee a specific answer. Be cautious of anyone who promises you will be named, ranked, or recommended by an AI assistant.

Glossary

Plain-language definitions for the terms used in this guide.

AEO
Answer engine optimization — the practice of shaping your online presence so AI assistants can find, understand, and recommend you.
GEO
Generative engine optimization — a near-synonym for AEO, focused on generative AI systems. Google's guidance treats both as extensions of SEO.
Answer engine
A system that returns a direct, synthesized answer to a question rather than a list of links — for example ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, or Google AI Overviews.
AI Overviews
Google's AI-generated summary shown above some search results. It does not appear for every query.
RAG
Retrieval-augmented generation — the technique of retrieving relevant passages from an index and grounding a generated answer in them, rather than relying on the model's memory alone.
Citation
A reference from an answer back to a source it used. Being cited means an assistant relied on your page.
Entity
A distinct thing the machine can recognize — in this context, you as a specific agent, with a name, role, brokerage, and service areas.
Structured data / schema
Standardized, machine-readable markup (often JSON-LD) that labels what a page is about. Google recommends JSON-LD, though it is not required for AI features.
Crawler
A program that visits and indexes pages on the web — for example Googlebot, OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, or PerplexityBot. If a crawler is blocked, its content cannot be used.
Share of voice
Your portion of all mentions within a tracked set of questions, compared with competitors.
Visibility
The share of tracked questions where you appear in the answer at all, regardless of rank or recommendation.
